Abstract

Effective information extraction remains a central challenge in Computer Vision (CV). While Transformer- and Mamba-based backbones have significantly advanced this field by providing powerful long-range modeling capability, they inherit architectural designs from Natural Language Processing (NLP) that may not optimally align with the inherent spatial structure of 2D visual representations. Recent progress has highlighted the potential of Fourier Neural Operator (FNO), which, with its favorable quasi-linear complexity and strong global modeling capacity, offers a promising alternative for visual representation learning; however, FNO exhibits fundamental limitations in capturing local high-frequency patterns due to the over-smoothing effect and bandwidth bottleneck.

To address these limitations, we propose Vision Filter (ViF), a generic backbone for CV, consisting of two synergistic components: Adaptive Modulation (AM) for flexibly enhancing sensitivity to high-frequency components in the frequency domain, and Selective Activation (SA) for dynamically balancing local time-domain and global frequency-domain information flow. Extensive experiments demonstrate that ViF consistently outperforms prominent variants of Transformer- and Mamba-based backbones across diverse visual tasks, including image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. ViF achieves lower computational complexity than Transformer-based models and better structural modeling than Mamba-based models, which suffer from spatial disruption due to their directional scanning mechanism. The joint time- and frequency-domain mechanism introduced in ViF establishes a promising paradigm for effective visual representation learning, bridging local high-frequency information with global low-frequency information.

Key Contributions

  • We propose Fourier Neural Filter (FNF), the first unified backbone that couples time-domain and frequency-domain analysis, inherently preserving the spatial structure of 2D visual representation.
  • We theoretically and empirically demonstrate that our proposed FNF resolves the inherent over-smoothing effect and bandwidth bottleneck of the original FNO.
  • The proposed model ViF achieves state-of-the-art performance on three mainstream visual tasks: ImageNet-1K classification, COCO object detection, and ADE20K semantic segmentation.

Method Overview

Our Fourier Neural Filter (FNF) extends the standard FNO by introducing an input-dependent kernel function that enables selective activation of local time-domain and global frequency-domain information flow through Hadamard product operations. Architecture

Our ViF model is structured into four hierarchical stages, mirroring the design principles of established vision backbones. The architecture consists of:

  • Stem Layer: Overlapped convolution for initial feature extraction
  • Four Stages: Each stage contains multiple ViF blocks with down-sampling layers
  • ViF Block: Comprises FNF module and FFN module with residual connections
  • FNF Module: Two-branch design with local and global convolutions

Key Innovation: Unlike FNO which uses a fixed integral kernel, FNF employs an input-dependent kernel that adaptively modulates information flow between time and frequency domains, addressing the over-smoothing effect and bandwidth bottleneck.

Theoretical Contributions

We provide rigorous theoretical analysis of FNO limitations and demonstrate how our proposed FNF addresses these fundamental issues through mathematical formulations and proofs.

Limitations of Fourier Neural Operator (FNO)

Proposition 1 Bandwidth Bottleneck

Consider a periodic function v expanded in a Fourier series. Let PK denote the projection onto Fourier modes {|k| ≤ K}. Any FNO layer FK(v) with fixed bandwidth K depends only on PK. If v is non-bandlimited, and the operator 𝒯 is not strictly low-pass, leading to an irreducible truncation error in the frequency domain:

infFK ‖FK(v) - 𝒯(v)‖ ≥ ‖PK⊥ 𝒯(v)‖
Proof sketch: FNO applies a fixed spectral map on {|k| ≤ K} and discards {|k| > K}. Therefore, two inputs with identical PK cannot be distinguished. The error lower bound follows from orthogonal decomposition into PK and PK⊥ components.
Proposition 2 Over-smoothing Effect

Let Mℓ(k) be the per-layer spectral multipliers on {|k| ≤ K}. If there exists ρ ∈ (0,1) and k0 ≤ K such that |Mℓ(k)| ≤ ρ for all |k| ≥ k0 and all layers ℓ, then the overall frequency response HL(k) = ∏ℓ=1L Mℓ(k) can satisfy:

|HL(k)| ≤ ρL → 0
on {|k| ≥ k0} as L → ∞

This leads to an over-smoothing spatial representation.

Proof sketch: Multiplicative contraction on the mid-/high-frequency modes accumulates exponentially with depth; coupled with the hard truncation outside {|k| ≤ K}, the output energy is concentrated in the low-frequency modes, while the high-frequency modes are progressively suppressed.

Fundamentals of Fourier Neural Filter (FNF)

Definition 1 FNO Integral Kernel

FNO is defined via a fixed integral kernel operator:

(K v)(x) = ∫D κ(x, y) v(y) dy

where κ: D × D → ℝ is the kernel function and v: D → ℝ is the input function. Through the Fourier transform, FNO can be formulated in the frequency domain as:

(K v)(x) = ℱ-1( Rφ · ℱ(v) )(x)

where Rφ = ℱ(κ) denotes the parameterized frequency-domain kernel.

Definition 2 FNF Adaptive Integral Kernel

FNF can be defined through an adaptive integral kernel operator:

(K v)(x) = ∫D κ(x, y; v) v(y) dy

where κ(x, y; v) is the input-dependent kernel function. In the implementation, FNF can also be formulated as:

(K v)(x) = T( G(v) ⊙ P(v) )(x)
P(v)(x) = ℱ-1( Rφ · ℱ(H(v)) )(x)

where G(v), H(v), and T(v) denote linear transforms, and ⊙ is the Hadamard product operation.

Remark 1: The fundamental distinction between FNO and FNF lies in their kernel functions: FNO employs a fixed kernel κ(x, y), whereas FNF applies an input-dependent kernel κ(x,y;v), enabling adaptive information flow modulation between time-domain and frequency-domain.
Definition 3 Selective Activation

The selective activation operates an element-wise multiplication in the time domain; in the frequency domain, this operation is mathematically equivalent to the convolution operation:

ℱ( G(v) ⊙ P(v) )(ω) = ( Ĝ(v) * P̂(v) )(ω)

This can be viewed as approximate magnitude modulation and phase addition:

(G(v) ⊙ P(v))i ≈ |G(v)i| · |P(v)i| · ei(θG(v)i + θP(v)i)
Remark 2: Selective activation achieves joint time–frequency modulation: it enhances informative mid-/high-frequency components while suppressing redundant low-frequency ones, alleviating the over-smoothing effect and bandwidth bottleneck of FNO.
Definition 4 Adaptive Modulation

The adaptive modulation operates through an amplitude-sensitive weighting function to achieve frequency balancing:

ℳ(z) = z ⊙ [ β · ‖z‖α ]

where ‖z‖ represents the magnitude of complex-valued input z, and α, β are learnable parameters.

Remark 3: When α < 1, the power-law weighting compresses the dynamic range between frequency components, effectively attenuating dominant low-frequency components while relatively enhancing weak high-frequency components. The adaptive parameter β provides global scaling control.

Related Work

Vision Transformer

Building on the success of Vision Transformer (ViT), subsequent developments have focused on making it more efficient through hierarchical designs like Swin Transformer, PVT, and NAT, establishing ViT as a fundamental architecture for diverse visual tasks.

Vision Mamba

Recent work on Vision Mamba aims to overcome fundamental limitations of directional scanning for 2D visual information processing, including bi-directional and quad-directional scanning approaches.

Fourier Transform for Vision

Previous work has successfully integrated Fourier transform into deep learning systems. GFNet achieves competitive performance with logarithmic linear complexity by replacing self-attention with 2D discrete Fourier transform.
